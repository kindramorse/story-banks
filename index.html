How to use this

Each panel below is one core story from your life and work. Open it and you'll find the story told once in full, then a set of formats: a carousel, a conviction post, a talking-head reel, a single static image, a b-roll caption. Same story, different shape, different job.

Pick the format that fits the week. Telling a full story? Use the carousel or reel. Want to plant a flag on what you believe? Use the conviction post or static line. They all pull from the same well.

This document grows. We add a new core story each time we find one, and you keep it after we're done working together. It's yours.

One quick convention. Any line shown in a framed box is text that goes ON the image or video. The grey box beneath it is the caption you paste into the post.

01 Why I Walked Away
Full story · all formats ▶
The Story

At a high-volume firm, he watched a case worth millions get readied for a $135,000 settlement that wouldn't even cover the client's medical bills. He said, out loud, that it was wrong. Around the same time his daughter was born and he asked for three weeks to be home with her. That was the moment it turned: his cases started disappearing from his desk, his paralegal was reassigned, and the message was fall in line or else. So he walked. He gave up an estimated quarter million to half a million dollars in cases he'd already built, with a mortgage, two kids, and no idea where the next paycheck would come from. He was 40. It felt like his last chance to do it right.

The trait it reveals: he does the right thing when it costs him everything. Villain: the volume model that treats people like inventory, never a named firm or person.

02 The Silver Platter
Full story · all formats ▶
The Story

Early in his career, a newspaper wrote that the only reason he had his job as a rookie deputy city attorney was his last name, that he should go earn his spurs somewhere his father wasn't calling the shots. To take that job, he'd already given up his own small firm and the clients paying him. There were meetings among city officials about whether he should keep it at all. The man who recruited him said, "Well, I guess we'll find out if you still have a job." He figured he'd be gone within the week.

What no one saw: his dad coached him his whole childhood and expected more of him than anyone on the team. He once hit a home run, and the whole drive home, all his dad talked about was the throw he missed from shortstop. He didn't fully understand it as a kid, but it was teaching him that you earn your place every time and nobody hands it to you. So being told he hadn't earned this one wasn't new. He didn't wait for the doubt to lift. He assumed everyone was waiting for him to mess up, and made sure he didn't. He won or settled every case he had there, negotiated the big deals, and argued an appellate motion most lawyers never get to touch. He won that too. The doubt never really flipped. He just out-worked it until there was nothing left to say.

The trait it reveals: he does his best work when he's underestimated, and he credits the father who raised him to earn every inch. Villain: public assumption, never a person.

06 Why You Haven't Heard From Your Lawyer
Conviction · stance, not a story ▶
The Stance

This one isn't a story, it's a belief he'll plant his feet on. Most firms sit on a case for almost two years before they really move, because the more files open, the more invoices out, the less work done. The waiting isn't an accident, it's the business model.

The mechanism most people never hear: before a suit is filed, your case sits with a mid-level adjuster who runs your injury through a computer program that spits out a number, like an order screen at a drive-through. The day a suit is filed, it leaves that desk. A manager weighs in, the other side has to bring in a real attorney, and the number moves. He files fast, because that's the quickest way to get the most before evidence quietly disappears.

The trait it reveals: he refuses to let people get parked. Villain: the business model, never a named firm.
