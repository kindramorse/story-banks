The through line

You gave it in one word, ten minutes in.

Redemption.

"Through the Lord, I have been redeemed. He has been there the whole time, even though I wasn't always there with him."

Everything below is one of two movements. Either the years you were the authority in your own life, or the moment you handed that over. The reader never gets told you've been redeemed. They watch it happen and reach it themselves, which is the only way anybody ever believes it.

House rules I held to throughout: no em dashes, the villain is always a force and never a person, and every caption ends on a moment instead of an explanation of what the moment meant. That last one will feel wrong the first few times. Trust it.

People keep telling me AI is going to wipe out my industry.

Caption

Go back far enough and families stuck together. We took care of our old people. The little kids and the old people were in the same room and it did something to both of them. The old ones helped the marriages stay together. The young ones gave the old ones a reason to still be here.

We traded that for independence and I am not convinced we came out ahead.

I'm not going back to eating chaos to keep the peace. That is not on the table and it never will be again.

But if there were a version where I got to protect my family and my kids still got their grandparents, I would take it today. Nobody has built that version yet.

So I'm sitting here holding both. Boundaries in one hand, and a table I still believe in with the other.

There's a version of this where I sound like a kid who finally stood up for himself.

And it worked. It never happened again. I was honestly surprised.

That lesson was excellent. I ran on it for the next twenty years. Anything came at me, I got bigger, louder, harder. In relationships. At work. In my own head. It kept working, which is exactly why I never questioned it.

Safe and armored are not the same thing. I did not learn that until I was forty.

I'm not proud of that story. I've never told it out loud. But I remember it vividly, and I know there's a fourteen year old somewhere right now about to learn the same excellent, expensive lesson.

I still catch myself on the stairs.
