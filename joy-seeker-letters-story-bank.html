We're all terminal

Ready · carries her mom, let it breathe

From her mom, who lived twelve years with terminal cancer and kept laughing.

Three hooks · pick by placement

ContextMy mom lived twelve years with terminal cancer and laughed more than anyone I have known.

ContrarianMy mom was the most alive person in every room, and she was the one who was dying.

IntrigueWhenever I got scared about my mom's diagnosis, she would laugh at me. It took me years to understand why.

My mom had terminal cancer for twelve years. Whenever I got scared about it, she would just laugh at me. "Wendy," she'd say. "We're all terminal, the day we're born." She was not being grim. She was the most alive person I knew. She just understood, sooner than the rest of us, that no day is promised. So she refused to waste the ones she had being afraid.

CaptionMy mom lived twelve years knowing how her story would end, and she spent them laughing more than anyone I have ever met. Whenever I would spiral, she would tease me. We are all terminal, she'd say, the day we're born. It took me most of my life to understand she was handing me a gift. None of us gets a guarantee. So the joy is not something to save for later. It is the whole point of right now. Share this with someone who needs to stop waiting.

Questions that pull better copy

Her words beat anything written from scratch. Send one question, ask for a raw two to three minute voice memo, then shape her actual lines into the moment-first format. It also takes the writing off her on the weeks she is depleted.

Drop into the moment

  • Take me into a specific moment from this week. Where were you standing, what time was it, what were you holding?
  • What is a sentence someone once said to you that you have never been able to forget, and where were you?
  • Tell me about a moment in your chair that you still think about.

The veins

  • What do women tell you in your chair that they do not say anywhere else?
  • What has changed about your own body this year that nobody warned you about?
  • When is the last time you felt invisible, and what exactly happened?
  • What is something you used to love, set down years ago, and have quietly picked back up?
  • In your own plain words, what does your faith give you on the hardest days?

What do you know now

  • What do you know now about joy, or loss, or your own worth, that you did not at thirty?
  • What would you say to the woman you were ten years ago?
  • What is the most rebellious thing a woman our age can do?
