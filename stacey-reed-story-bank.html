Stop living in a fantasy

Post this first. Everything else in this bank is downstream of that room, and it answers the question every relationship coach's audience is quietly holding: how would you know?

Format + dialStory post. Run hot for reach.
B-rollA room with chairs in rows. Walking into a building. Sitting down. Nothing literal, this one is carried by the talking head.

Hooks text on screen

  • ContextI paid to be in that room. I raised my hand. I was the one who wanted to get picked.
  • ContrarianThe best thing anyone ever did for me was humiliate me in front of seventy six people.
  • IntrigueShe listened to me answer one question and said seven words back. I have not been the same person since.

Caption

There were seventy seven of us in that room and I wanted to be picked.

I was green and I was thrilled to be there. Hand up. Me, pick me.

She asked me a question and I answered it the way I answered everything back then. Fluff, fluff, fluff, fluff, fluff. Something like: I've got a couple of jobs, I'm also a consultant, I've got my two boys, I'm going through a divorce but we're doing pretty good, and I'm getting a restraining order, so I'm taking care of myself.

And she said, stop living in a fucking fantasy.

Seventy six people turned and looked at me.

I was embarrassed. I want to be honest about that. But the embarrassment is not what got me. What got me is that I sat there and actually asked the question. Am I living in a fantasy?

I had no mutual relationships. I didn't have any of the things I said I wanted. I did not mean what I said or say what I meant. I did not have one leg to stand on.

How did she read me? I had been doing it so long I thought I was getting away with it.

It was the mask, the smile, the humor. It wasn't even a lie I was telling. It was just how I did things.

She coached me for another minute and then I went back to my seat and somebody else got a turn.

And I sat in that chair thinking, I am about to find out a lot more than I bargained for. And I'm scared.

DIGWhat she actually asked you. You gave me what you probably said back, but not the question. And where the room was, what year, what city. One concrete detail at the top puts the reader in the chair next to you.

Caption

My sister is eight years older than me and she left when she was about sixteen. From then on we were both on our own.

She had a story about me and I had a story about her.

Hers was that I was the baby who got the good version. The one who got kept. The spoiled one. She had her reasons for believing it.

Mine was that we had nothing in common and only had each other because of our mother.

Years later, sober, I went back and told her what my life had actually been. Not the highlights. The real thing.

And she said, oh my God. I had no idea. I told myself you were spoiled and had it good.

We had been sisters her whole adult life and neither of us had any idea what the other one had lived through.

She has been a different person with me since. Not because she forgave me for anything. Because she did not know what she did not know.

Where the stories aren't finished

Eleven DIG markers in this document. Eight of them are in the nine posts you can run today and three are in the holds. Every one is the same problem, and it is not a writing problem.

You told me the summary and not the scene. You know you do it, because you said it about yourself on the call: “it's just hard for somebody like me who is a maximalist, I want to give you everything.” The maximalism is not the problem. The problem is that giving everything comes out as the shape of a decade instead of one Tuesday in it.

Every one of those markers is a single room away from finished, and nobody on earth can supply that room but you. Sit with any one of them for ten minutes and it will come.

Six more stories surfaced that I could not write yet, because each one is still a summary:

  • The six trees. Your sister has six Christmas trees, she rotates the designs, and it is the biggest deal in her year. You learned that this week, at almost eight years sober, and your reaction was “I didn't even know that.” That is a whole post about what listening actually buys you, and it is the smallest and quietest thing in the entire hour. It needs her yes, and it needs the December plans left out of it.
  • “I respect you so much. I wish I could do the things you do.” Your sister said that to you and your answer was “me?” That post is not about her, it is about the gap between what you have built and what you can still receive, and you have never told it.
  • The two jobs and the restraining order. That entire sentence from Post 1 is a life summarized in one breath. Any single clause in it is a post on its own.
  • How you picked the three words. Loving, trusting, committed. You said they were the three hardest things in the world for you, and then you moved straight on. How you landed on those three and not three easier ones is a story and you skipped it.
  • The amends. “I made an amends after I got sober” was one clause and you kept going. That is almost always where the ore is.
  • The plan instead of the list. You have the distinction and no story attached to it. The distinction is not postable. The first time you ran your own life off a plan instead of a to do list is.

What I left out on purpose

  • Your mother's own history. What she says about her daughters, what you think it costs her. You called it a black hole of her own making, and it may well be true. It puts the villain on a person, so it is out of every post here.
  • Your ex. Everywhere except one clause in one held post. Nothing about the abuse, nothing about what he did, nothing that would let anybody identify him.
  • Your stepchildren, except as people getting their own photographs back.
  • The four levels of responsibility. It is a good distinction and it is genuinely useful in a session. On the page it turns a story into a framework and the reader stops watching and starts studying. Save it for the room.
  • Anything about a client. Nothing in these posts stands on somebody else's session.
  • Any number attached to money. Not one post here rests on an income claim.
